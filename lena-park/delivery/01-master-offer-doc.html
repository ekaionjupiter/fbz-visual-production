1. Executive Summary

The situation

Dr. Lena Park runs a private 1:1 pediatric sleep practice under the informal name Quiet Hours. She holds a PhD in developmental psychology, is a certified pediatric sleep consultant (2019), and has run her practice since 2020. She is booked about seven weeks out and turns families away every week. Her 1:1 packages are $650 (3 weeks) and $1,100 (6 weeks). Clients come from Instagram (about 8,000 followers, @quiethours.sleep), an email list of about 1,200 subscribers, and referrals from two local pediatric practices and a lactation consultant.

The problem she wants to solve is not a revenue problem first. It is a reach problem. The families she turns away are, in her words, "usually the ones who need the basics most," and the basics are what she repeats "word for word, practically" on every first and second call.

The opportunity

Package those basics as a self-paced course so any first-time parent can get them, on their phone, in the dark, without waiting seven weeks. The basics already have a name and a structure: The Quiet Hours Foundations (Rhythm, Room, Response, Relay, Recovery). They already have signature tools: The Evening Rhythm, The Pause and Read, and The Night Relay. And they already have a voice that parents trust: calm, steady, unhurried, and honest about what she is and is not.

The offer

First 90 Nights: a self-paced video mini-course. A Start Here orientation plus five short modules (one per Foundation), lessons of 5 to 12 minutes, about 2.5 hours total, printable worksheets, a recorded quarterly Answers video built from submitted questions, lifetime access, and a 14-day no-questions refund. No community and no live calls in version 1.

  • Regular price: $147
  • Founding price: $97, waitlist only, November 4 to November 8, 2026, ending 11:59 p.m. Eastern on Sunday, November 8
  • 1:1 credit: the full course price is credited toward a 1:1 package if the buyer upgrades within 60 days

The pre-launch plan in one line

Open a waitlist on October 14, 2026 with a new lead magnet (The Evening Rhythm Guide), warm it with four calm emails, open doors on November 4, 2026 at the founding price for the waitlist only, and close the founding window quietly on Sunday, November 8 before the holidays.

The strategic bet

The pediatric sleep space online is loud. Lena's own biggest fear is that her launch "will feel like every other loud sleep program on Instagram." That is the opening. Quiet Hours wins by being the opposite: fewer rules, fewer decisions, no countdown timers, no promises she cannot keep, and a clear line between education and medical care. The restraint is the positioning.

What success looks like for this build

  1. Lena approves every claim, name, and price in this document without having to rewrite her own voice.
  2. The waitlist page, lead magnet, emails, and ads all sound like the same calm person.
  3. Nothing in the launch could be read as medical advice, a guarantee, or a sleep outcome promise.
  4. A tired parent reading on a phone with one hand can understand the offer in under a minute.

2. Origin Story

2.1 Long form (in Lena's voice)

For use on the sales page "About Lena" section, the welcome email, and as the source for story-based posts. Edit lightly; do not add events that are not here.

I'll start with a confession. I was terrible at this with my own baby.

My first daughter, Mina, was born in 2015. I was in the last year of my PhD in developmental psychology. My dissertation was about infant routines and parental stress. I had read everything. I could cite the papers.

Then Mina arrived, and she did not sleep in any way the papers described. At 3 a.m., nothing I knew helped me at all.

I remember sitting on the bathroom floor around 4 a.m. with her finally asleep on my chest. I was trying to google "is it normal for a baby to," and I couldn't finish typing the sentence. My husband, Daniel, was asleep in the next room. Not because he didn't care. Because neither of us knew what his job was.

I felt like a fraud.

Around week three, our midwife came for a home visit. I was a mess. She sat down on the couch and said, very calmly, "Let's make tonight smaller."

Not the whole month. Not sleeping through the night. Just tonight. What is one thing we do at 7 p.m. that is the same as yesterday?

That sentence rewired me.

The next thing I did was the least sophisticated thing I have ever made. I took an index card and wrote our evening on it. Bath or wash. Feed. Dim lights. One book. One song. Down. Underneath, I wrote who does what after midnight. Daniel took the first stretch. I took the second. We taped it to the fridge.

It worked better than my dissertation. Not because Mina suddenly slept. She didn't, not for a while. But we stopped improvising. We stopped fighting at 2 a.m. We had a rhythm, not a schedule.

When my son, Theo, was born in 2018, I used the index card from day one. Friends started asking for it. Then friends of friends. I certified as a pediatric sleep consultant in 2019 and opened my practice in 2020, mostly by accident. People found me on Instagram during lockdown, when so many parents were alone with a newborn and no grandparents could visit.

Today my calendar is booked weeks out, and I turn families away every week. That part bothers me. The families I can't take are usually the ones who need the basics most. And the basics are what I say on every first and second call, almost word for word.

So I put them in one place. First 90 Nights is the index card, done properly, for the families who can't get on my calendar.

I'm not a medical doctor. I'm a doctor of developmental psychology and a certified pediatric sleep consultant, and I'm careful about that line. What I can do is help you build calm, predictable evenings, a plan for the night that both of you understand, and a little more room to breathe.

You are not doing it wrong. Let's make tonight smaller.

When my first baby arrived, I had a PhD in infant routines and parental stress, and at 3 a.m. none of it helped. What did help was a midwife who said "Let's make tonight smaller," and an index card on our fridge with our evening order and who covered which stretch of the night. Quiet Hours and First 90 Nights are that index card, done properly, for parents who can't wait weeks for a spot on my calendar.

I'm a developmental psychologist and certified pediatric sleep consultant who learned, on a bathroom floor at 4 a.m., that tired parents don't need more rules; they need fewer decisions after dark.

3. Market and Positioning

3.1 Category

Primary category: Online infant sleep education for new parents. Sub-category Quiet Hours owns: Gentle, foundations-first newborn sleep education for first-time parents in the first months, built around an evening rhythm and a shared night plan, with no sleep training protocol.

First 90 Nights is not a sleep training course. It is not a medical program. It is a foundations course: the steady, practical groundwork a parent sets in the early months before anything falls apart.

3.2 The gap

What first-time parents currently find (as described by Lena from her client work):

  • Tracking apps that log every minute and mostly make parents more anxious.
  • Big sleep programs that feel like boot camp.
  • Wake window charts that are fine as a reference but get treated like law.
  • Gadgets, bought in hope.
  • Relatives' advice (including the mother-in-law).
  • Forums at 3 a.m., full of contradictory advice.

What they all have in common: they hand the parent more data or more rules. Lena's diagnosis: "Tired people don't need more rules. They need fewer decisions."

The gap Quiet Hours fills: a calm, credible, low-pressure place for first-time parents to learn the foundations early, with a plan both partners can follow, from someone who is clear about the line between education and medical care.

For first-time parents in the third trimester or the first months with a new baby, who feel lost at night and are drowning in contradictory advice, Quiet Hours is a calm, foundations-first approach to newborn sleep that replaces guesswork with a simple evening rhythm, a way to respond at night, and a night plan both partners understand. Unlike loud sleep programs, tracking apps, and strict training protocols, Quiet Hours reduces the number of decisions you make after dark, never shames, never promises outcomes, and is taught by a developmental psychologist and certified pediatric sleep consultant who is clear that she is not a medical doctor.

3.5 DO NOT CLAIM list

Every writer, designer, editor, and ad reviewer working on Quiet Hours must check copy against this list. If a line comes close to any of these, cut it.

Medical and safety

  1. Do not say or imply that Quiet Hours, First 90 Nights, or Lena provides medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment.
  2. Do not say anything reduces, prevents, or lowers the risk of SIDS or any sleep-related infant death. Safe sleep content only points to current AAP guidance and the family's pediatrician.
  3. Do not give safe sleep instructions as Lena's own rules. Phrase as "follow current AAP guidance and talk to your pediatrician."
  4. Do not diagnose or name conditions (reflux, colic, sleep apnea, tongue tie, allergies, and so on) or suggest that the course addresses them.
  5. Do not give feeding advice (amounts, intervals, night weaning, formula or breastfeeding guidance). Feeding questions go to the pediatrician and, where relevant, a lactation consultant.
  6. Do not claim to treat, prevent, or manage postpartum depression, anxiety, or any parental mood condition. Recovery content may only encourage parents to reach out to their own doctor.
  7. Do not recommend any product, device, swaddle, sleep positioner, monitor, or gadget as safe or effective.

Outcomes and guarantees

  1. No guarantees of any kind. Never use the word "guaranteed" (the only guarantee-adjacent term allowed is the 14-day refund, described as a refund, not a guarantee of results).
  2. No promise that a baby will "sleep through the night," at any age or by any week.
  3. No promise of specific hours of sleep, fewer night wakings, longer stretches, or faster settling.
  4. No "results in X nights" or "in just X days" language. The product name "First 90 Nights" describes a period of life, not a timeline to a result. Never write "sleeping well in 90 nights."
  5. No before-and-after outcome claims beyond the six approved testimonials, used verbatim.

Evidence language

  1. Do not use "science-backed," "evidence-based," "proven," "clinically proven," "research-proven," or "studies show" as marketing phrases. Lena finds this sloppy, even as a researcher.
  2. No statistics, percentages, or numbers about infant sleep, parent sleep, or outcomes unless Lena supplies the source in writing.
  3. No study citations in marketing copy.
  4. Do not say "over 60 families" or any client count in public copy unless Lena approves the exact phrasing. (She has about 60 written testimonials; permission exists for 6.)

Credentials

  1. Lena is not a medical doctor. Never use "pediatrician," "physician," "MD," "medical expert," or "sleep doctor" to describe her.
  2. When "Dr. Lena Park" appears on any page, the same page must state that her doctorate is a PhD in developmental psychology and that she is not a medical doctor (use the compliance disclaimer).
  3. Do not name the university, lab, or certifying organization unless Lena supplies and approves them. [VERIFY]
  4. Do not say she "studied sleep" for her PhD. Her dissertation was on infant routines and parental stress. Say exactly that.
  5. Do not inflate "coordinated research in a university infant development lab" into "led," "directed," or "ran a lab."

Proof and relationships

  1. Do not name, describe, or hint at the two referral pediatric practices or the lactation consultant. No "trusted by pediatricians," "pediatrician-recommended," or "recommended by local doctors."
  2. No "as seen in" or press language. There is no press.
  3. Use only the six approved testimonials, verbatim, with first name and baby age only. No paraphrasing, combining, or trimming that changes meaning.

Shame, fear, and hype

  1. No shaming language: "ruining your baby's sleep," "bad habits," "creating sleep problems," "you're doing it wrong," "spoiling," "fix your baby."
  2. No fear-based hooks that suggest lasting harm from current choices (for example "the mistake that ruins sleep for years").
  3. Banned words: "hack," "sleep training boot camp," "guaranteed," "fix your baby."
  4. Do not present "drowsy but awake" as a magic rule or "self-soothe" as a pass or fail test.
  5. Do not frame the course as "cry it out" or sleep training. Do not describe the Pause and Read as "letting them cry."
  6. No countdown timers, flashing urgency, "last chance!!" subject lines, or fake scarcity. The founding window is real and dated; state it calmly.

8.3 How the names relate

  • Quiet Hours is the feeling: the hush of evening, the hours you are trying to protect.
  • The Foundations are the method. They live in every product. A parent who learns the five R's in a carousel will meet the same five R's in the course and on a 1:1 call.
  • The tools are the things a parent actually does. Each tool belongs to one Foundation. Room and Recovery do not have a named tool in v1; that is intentional, to keep the system small.
  • The Evening Rhythm Guide is the free taste of the first tool. It leads naturally into First 90 Nights, which teaches all five Foundations.
  • First 90 Nights names a season of life (the first months), not a result. It sits under Quiet Hours, never as a separate brand.
  • 1:1 Support is the deepest level. Course buyers who upgrade within 60 days get the course price credited.

8.4 Naming rules

  1. Always write Quiet Hours as two words, title case. Never "QuietHours" or "Quiet Hours Sleep" in body copy.
  2. On first mention per page, pair the brand and the person: "Quiet Hours, with Dr. Lena Park" or the full descriptor.
  3. "Dr. Lena Park" never appears on a page without the compliance disclaimer (or, in short formats like ads, at minimum "PhD, developmental psychology; certified pediatric sleep consultant; not a medical doctor" in caption or landing page).
  4. In casual and voice-led copy, "Lena" is fine.
  5. Always "The Quiet Hours Foundations" on first mention; "the Foundations" after that.
  6. The five Foundations are always listed in order: Rhythm, Room, Response, Relay, Recovery. Capitalize them when used as Foundation names.
  7. Tool names always take "The": The Evening Rhythm, The Pause and Read, The Night Relay. Capitalized.
  8. Do not create new branded terms in launch copy without adding them to Section 9 and getting approval.
  9. First 90 Nights: numerals, no hyphen, no "The." Never "First 90 Days" or "90 Nights Program."
  10. Never pair "First 90 Nights" with a result (for example "90 nights to better sleep").
  11. Never use "sleep training," "boot camp," "method that works," or "system" language that implies a protocol.
