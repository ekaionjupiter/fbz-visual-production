Why it feels so hard

You have plenty of information. What you don't have is a plan.

You did the reading. You took the hospital class. Then the baby arrived, and none of it seemed to apply at 3 a.m.

The apps that track every minute.

More data, logged at 2 a.m., and somehow more worry.

The wake window charts.

Fine as a reference. Exhausting when they start to feel like law.

The forums and the family advice.

Every answer contradicts the last one. Each hands you more rules.

But tired people don't need more rules. They need fewer decisions.

When every night is improvised, every waking turns into a debate. Feed or wait? Pick up or pat? Whose turn is it? Is this normal? That is exhausting long before you count the hours of sleep.

A different starting point

Rhythm, not schedule.

A schedule is clock times. Newborns don't read clocks.

A rhythm is the order things happen in. Same steps, same sequence, every evening. You can always do the order, even on a terrible day.

Fewer decisions after dark

Decide in daylight, so you don't have to decide at 3 a.m.

  • You know your evening order before evening comes.
  • You know what to do when your baby wakes.
  • You know whose turn it is.

Nothing dramatic. Small things, in order, every night.

Foundations
The framework

The Quiet Hours Foundations

Five foundations. Each one takes a few decisions off your plate.

01Foundation one

Rhythm

An evening order you can follow half asleep.

The same steps in the same sequence, roughly 20 to 40 minutes each night. The index card on the fridge, done properly, with a “good enough” version for the hard nights.

02Foundation two

Room

A sleep space that's ready before you're exhausted.

Safe sleep basics as published by the AAP, plus light, sound, and temperature. Set it up once, in daylight, and check the details with your pediatrician.

03Foundation three

Response

A calm way to meet night wakings.

The Pause and Read: a breath, a look at what you're actually seeing, then a response to that instead of to the panic. Not “let them cry.” Look before you leap.

04Foundation four

Relay

A night plan both of you understand.

Your Night Relay: who covers which stretch, what the handoff looks like, and what the off-duty person gets to do. Sleep, without guilt. A version for single parents, too.

05Foundation five

Recovery

Care for the parent, not just the baby.

Your rest, your nervous system, your expectations. And when to reach out: your pediatrician for your baby, your own doctor for you.

Is this for you?

Made for the early months.

First 90 Nights is for you if:

  • You're a first-time parent, in your third trimester or with a baby under about 4 months.
  • You want a calm plan before things start to feel like they're falling apart.
  • You're tired of contradictory advice and want one steady approach.
  • You have a partner who wants to help but hasn't been given a job.
  • You're parenting solo and want a plan that protects your own rest.
  • You're going back to work soon and want your evenings to feel predictable.

It's not the right fit if:

  • You have medical concerns about your baby's sleep or breathing. Please start with your pediatrician.
  • You're looking for a strict sleep training protocol for an older baby.
  • You want a promise that your baby will sleep through the night by a certain week. I won't make that promise. Every baby is different.

Meet your guide

Hi, I'm Lena.

I have a PhD in developmental psychology. My dissertation was about infant routines and parental stress. So when my daughter Mina was born in 2015, I had read everything. I could cite the papers.

And at 3 a.m., none of it helped.

Our midwife came for a home visit around week three. She sat on the couch and said, very calmly, “Let's make tonight smaller.” Then I took an index card and wrote our evening on it. Bath or wash, feed, dim lights, one book, one song, down. We taped it to the fridge.

Mina didn't suddenly sleep. But we stopped improvising. We had a rhythm, not a schedule. Years later, after coordinating research in a university infant development lab, I certified as a pediatric sleep consultant in 2019 and opened my 1:1 practice in 2020.

One more thing, because it matters: I'm a doctor of developmental psychology, not a medical doctor. For anything about your baby's health, feeding, breathing, or safe sleep, your pediatrician is your person, and I'll point you there every time.

Questions

Questions parents ask

Is this medical advice?
No. First 90 Nights is educational. I have a PhD in developmental psychology and I'm a certified pediatric sleep consultant, but I'm not a medical doctor. I don't diagnose or treat. For anything about your baby's health, feeding, breathing, or safe sleep, please talk to your pediatrician and follow current AAP guidance.
My baby has reflux, or another medical issue. Can I still take it?
Please talk to your pediatrician first. If your pediatrician is comfortable with you working on evening rhythm and night routines, the course may be useful alongside their care. It is never a substitute for it.
When should I start?
The course was made for the third trimester through about 4 months. Many parents like to watch it before the baby arrives, while there's still time to think. Others start in the early weeks. Both work.
My baby is older than 4 months. Is it still for us?
The foundations apply at any age, especially Rhythm, Relay, and Recovery. But this isn't a sleep training program for older babies. If you're looking for a structured plan for an older baby, 1:1 support may be a better fit.
How much time does it take?
About two and a half hours in total. Lessons are 5 to 12 minutes, so you can watch one during a feed. You keep lifetime access, so there's no rush.
Will it work with my partner? I'm a single parent.
That's what the Relay module is for. You'll build a Night Relay together: who covers which stretch, how the handoff works, and how the off-duty person actually rests. If you're parenting solo, there's a version built around a support person, or a plan to protect your own stretch of rest.
Will my baby sleep through the night after this?
I can't promise that, and I'd be wary of anyone who does. Every baby is different. What the course gives you is a calm, predictable plan for your evenings and nights, so you're making fewer decisions after dark.
What if it isn't right for me?
You have 14 days to ask for a full refund, no questions asked. Just reply to any course email.
What if I want 1:1 help later?
If you buy First 90 Nights and decide within 60 days that you'd like 1:1 support, I'll credit the full course price toward a 1:1 package. You'll arrive with the basics already in place, so our time together goes further.
