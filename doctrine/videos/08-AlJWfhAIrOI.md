## Key takeaways

**Tools / models named**
- **Higgsfield MCP** (sponsor) gives Claude and ChatGPT direct access to image/video models inside the chat, no tab-hopping. Referral: https://higgsfield.ai/s/claude-opus-5-5-yt-josephmartin-myCGuC
- Models compared: **Claude Opus 5.5** vs **GPT-6 Astra**. Video generator under the hood: **Seedance 2.5** ("Cance/Cydance 2.5"), including its video-reference / scene-continuation feature.
- Voice-over: model asked user to select a voice, generated VO, Claude self-reviewed and regenerated bad lines before assembly.
- Misc affiliate: Epidemic Sound, Motion Array, VidIQ.

**Test workflow (4 rounds, identical prompts to both LLMs)**
1. Video prompting: intentionally vague request for a "30-second found footage horror sequence", leaving story/shot control to the LLM.
2. Scriptwriting: "write and animate a breakup scene".
3. Multi-step video creation: explainer video about the con man who sold the Eiffel Tower, "Vox style" image references, LLM searches web, picks voice, generates images, VO, edits autonomously.
4. Spatial logic: design a buildable Lego rubber duck with instruction PDF.

**Findings relevant to prompting generative media**
- Prompt detail drives output quality: Opus asked for a "recording dot on the footage", which "sells the found footage really well"; Astra did not ask, did not get it. Fill every second with instructed movement or you get "awkward pause[s]".
- Opus structured a story progression: exterior night shot, door-opening shot, night-vision switch, held found-footage POV even when characters run.
- Dialogue anti-slop rule: AI scripts "tend to write ... a ton of exposition" (characters narrating plot or thoughts), which "comes off as super corny ... Hallmark movie or soap operas". Good = leave "room to breathe", fewer lines, more real.
- Continuity rule: when splitting into two clips, cut to a genuinely different angle (e.g., close-up) or attach the first video as a video reference so the model knows what a reverse shot looks like; otherwise you get a jump cut.
- Opus used character and location references to keep two shots consistent.
- Multi-step: Opus delivered a captionless version unprompted; its explainer had slightly better animation and "more polished" feel; script more informative and VO more accurate because it QA'd itself.
- Lego test: Opus over-delivered an interactive 3D instruction interface (bricks animate per step), accurate parts list plus a search query to order parts; took ~20 min and burned more credits for not following the PDF instruction.
- Verdict: Opus 5.5 won 3 of 4 (lost breakup scene narrowly).
