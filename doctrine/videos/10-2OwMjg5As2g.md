### The six styles, with the exact prompting move for each
1. **Launch videos (SaaS/tech; $500–3,000 per 15 s).** Two methods:
   - *From scratch:* "paste my client's website link, upload the logo, and hit send." Claude analyzes "their entire product, core benefits, and target audience."
   - *From reference (1:1 recreation):* drop a reference video into Claude and "ask for the exact same one just from the reference, and it will create it one-to-one." Ask Claude to "write a prompt" from the reference first. Static images in the recreation can be told to animate for a 1:1 match.
   - Quality bar quoted: "look at the text. It's not melting, it's not warping, it's locked. And the motion still moves like a real watch ad."
   - **Credit-saving hack:** "Generate a nine-frame storyboard first to approve the layout and visuals beforehand. If something looks off, fix it at the image stage, then plug it in as a reference before rendering the video."
2. **Motion graphics on real footage ($200–500 per 60 s).** Upload a talking-head video into Claude; "ask for dynamic motion and camera moves"; give it a script or let Claude write one. Claude + MCP "starts building the design sheet. First, you pick a font" (use the brand font), approve, and generation starts. **Pro tip (fonts/typography): "Always ask for the design sheet in HTML. Fonts arrive way better that way. That's a trick for anyone who wants to keep their brand identity intact."** Output includes cloned voice and invented camera angles; graphics sync to speech.
3. **Hypermotion product ads (most in-demand across every niche).** "attach a photo of the product in Claude, and say you need a hypermotion ad." No product photo → generate one. Set the style in words: "expensive, minimal, bright" / "bright and dynamic". Psychology: "The same burger looks like a $5 burger or a $15 burger depending on how it moves on screen." Should look "like it was shot on a real camera."
4. **3D real estate flythroughs ($2,000–15,000+/min; cheap studios ≥$75/s; 7–8 weeks normally; beginners list $500–1,000 per property).** Input: a static architectural render (developers always have them). Prompt: "a 3D real estate motion video with camera fly-throughs, interior reveals, and text overlays showing pricing and square footage."
5. **2D explainers (agencies: $3,500–12,000 and 4–8 weeks per 60 s; beginners $300–800).** "I tell Claude what the product or the course is, who it's for, and what should be on the screen. He writes the prompt."
6. **Editorial explainers ($1,500–4,000/min).** "minutes of non-stop text, numbers, and charts. One wrong number and the whole video goes in the trash." Hand Claude the topic and the numbers yourself, or have it "research everything and verify the facts." Acceptance criteria: "every word is readable. The charts animate with actual context and the on-screen numbers match the voiceover."

### Anti-slop / quality rules extracted
- Text must be locked: no melting, no warping; typography should match "professional software" (that is what the motion prompt skill enforces).
- Keep brand identity: brand font, logo upload, design sheet in HTML before rendering.
- Approve a storyboard (image stage) before spending video credits; fix problems at the image stage.
- Numbers on screen must match voiceover; verify facts before rendering editorial pieces.
- Expect failed takes; iterate by re-prompting rather than opening editing software.

### Economics / client acquisition (for context)
- All six styles incl. failed takes: ~$70 / ~1,800 credits / one evening ≈ $5 per video vs. ≥$100 going rate.
- Find clients: freelance platforms (search "motion graphics ad"), pre-empt companies with public launch countdowns by sending a finished video, and post every output as portfolio/content.
